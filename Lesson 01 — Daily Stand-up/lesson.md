# Lesson 01 — Daily Stand-up

## 1. Lesson Overview

**Target Level:** Level 1  
**Target WPM:** 110–130 WPM  
**Meeting Type:** Daily Stand-up  
**IT Context:** Web Development / Sprint Work  
**Characters:** 2  
**Conversation Length:** 12–16 turns  

### Main Goal

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Understand common questions used in a daily stand-up.
- Give a short update about what you completed.
- Explain what you are currently working on.
- Mention a blocker clearly.
- Recognize common meeting phrases even when they are spoken naturally.

### Core Skill

> **Hear → Recognize → Understand → Respond**

The goal is not to memorize the dialogue.

The goal is to hear a question such as:

> “What are you working on today?”

and immediately understand:

> “Họ đang hỏi hôm nay mình đang làm gì.”

Then respond naturally.

---

# 2. Situation

It is Monday morning.

The development team has a short daily stand-up meeting.

The Project Manager wants to know:

1. What each developer completed.
2. What they are working on today.
3. Whether they have any blockers.

You are **David**, the Developer.

**Sarah** is the Project Manager.

---

# 3. IT Context

The team is working on an e-commerce website.

David is currently working on a product configuration feature.

Yesterday, he finished the backend configuration.

Today, he plans to work on the frontend and testing.

There is one possible blocker: he is waiting for the QA environment to be ready.

### Important

In real meetings, **update** does not only mean “cập nhật thông tin”.

For example:

> “Can you give me an update?”

usually means:

> “Cho tôi biết tình hình hiện tại như thế nào?”

---

# 5. Business Meeting Patterns

These are patterns you should recognize immediately.

### Asking for an update

**“Can I get an update on this?”**

= Cho tôi xin update về việc này được không?

**“How are we doing on this?”**

= Việc này đang tiến triển thế nào rồi?

**“Where are we on this?”**

= Việc này đang đến đâu rồi?

---

### Asking about current work

**“What are you working on today?”**

= Hôm nay bạn đang làm gì?

**“What are you working on now?”**

= Hiện tại bạn đang làm gì?

---

### Asking about blockers

**“Any blockers?”**

= Có vấn đề gì đang cản trở không?

**“Do you have any blockers?”**

= Bạn có blocker nào không?

---

# 6. Natural Meeting English

In a real meeting, people often use shorter expressions than textbook English.

For example:

### Textbook

> “Do you have any blockers?”

### Meeting English

> “Any blockers?”

Both mean essentially the same thing.

---

### Textbook

> “Can you give me an update about the task?”

### Meeting English

> “Can I get an update?”

The shorter version is very common in meetings.

---

### Textbook

> “What are you currently working on?”

### Meeting English

> “What are you working on?”

The word **currently** is often unnecessary because the context already tells us the question is about current work.

---

# 7. Natural / Reduced Speech

Do not try to hear every word separately.

Listen for the **sound group**.

### “Can I get an update?”

Natural speech may sound closer to:

> “Can I get an update?”

The words **can I** may be connected together.

---

### “What are you working on?”

In natural speech:

> “What are you working on?”

The important information is:

**working on**

---

### “Do you have any blockers?”

The important information is:

**any blockers**

You do not need to consciously translate every word.

Train yourself to recognize the whole pattern.

# 8. Conversation

### Sarah — Project Manager
Hey David, can I get a quick update on your task?

### David — Developer
Sure. I finished the backend configuration yesterday.

### Sarah

### Sarah

Sounds good.

### Sarah
Anything else from your side?

### David
No, that's all for me.

### Sarah
Alright, thanks, David.

# 13. Speaking Pattern — Daily Update

A simple daily update can follow this structure:

### Yesterday

> “Yesterday, I finished ___.”

### Today

> “Today, I'm working on ___.”

### Blocker

> “I'm waiting for ___.”

### Optional impact

> “It might delay ___.”

---

## Example

> Yesterday, I finished the backend configuration.  
> Today, I'm working on the frontend.  
> I'm waiting for the QA environment.  
> It might delay testing.

You do **not** need to give a long explanation.

A good stand-up update is usually:

**short + clear + relevant**
