# Lesson 02 — Giving a Work Update

## 1. Lesson Overview

**Target Level:** Level 1  
**Target WPM:** 110–130 WPM  
**Meeting Type:** Daily / Project Status Update  
**IT Context:** Web Development / Feature Development  
**Characters:** 2  
**Conversation Length:** 12–16 turns  

### Main Goal

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Understand different ways a PM asks for a work update.
- Explain what you have completed.
- Explain what you are currently working on.
- Give a simple progress update.
- Give a rough completion estimate.
- Understand common short expressions used during status discussions.

### Core Skill

> **Recognize the question → Identify what information is needed → Give a concise answer**

---

# 2. Situation

It is Tuesday morning.

Sarah, the Project Manager, wants to check the progress of a new checkout feature.

David, the Developer, worked on the feature yesterday and is continuing the work today.

The backend part is mostly complete.

The frontend still needs some work, and David expects to finish it by the end of the day.

---

# 3. IT Context

The team is developing a new checkout flow for an e-commerce website.

David is responsible for the implementation.

Current status:

- Backend: mostly completed
- Frontend: in progress
- Testing: not started yet
- Expected completion: today

The purpose of the conversation is not to solve a technical problem.

Sarah simply wants to understand:

> **Where are we on this?**

# 5. Business Meeting Patterns

## Asking for an update

### “Can I get an update on this?”

= Cho tôi xin update về việc này được không?

---

### “Where are we on this?”

= Việc này đang đến đâu rồi?

This is a very useful meeting expression.

Do not interpret **where** literally as a physical location.

---

### “How are we doing on this?”

= Việc này đang tiến triển thế nào?

---

### “How's the progress?”

= Tiến độ thế nào rồi?

---

## Asking about completion

### “Is it done?”

= Xong chưa?

### “Are we still on track?”

= Tiến độ vẫn đúng kế hoạch chứ?

---

# 6. Natural Meeting English

In meetings, people often avoid long formal questions.

For example:

### Formal

> “Could you please provide me with an update regarding the current status of the feature?”

### Natural meeting English

> “Can I get an update on this?”

The second version is much more useful for real-time listening practice.

---

Another example:

### Formal

> “Could you tell me how much progress you have made?”

### Meeting English

> “Where are we on this?”

Again, the meaning is understood from the meeting context.

---

# 7. Useful Response Patterns

When giving an update, use a simple structure.

### Completed work

> “I finished the backend yesterday.”

### Current work

> “I'm working on the frontend now.”

### Remaining work

> “I still need to finish the validation.”

### Expected completion

> “I should have it done today.”

---

# 8. Natural / Reduced Speech

Pay attention to these expressions in the audio.

### “How are we doing on this?”

The phrase:

> **How are we doing on this?**

should be recognized as one meeting pattern.

Do not try to translate every word individually.

---

### “I should have it done today.”

The important meaning is:

> **I expect to finish it today.**

**should** here does not mean a strict obligation.

It expresses an expectation.

---

### “I'm still working on it.”

The key phrase is:

> **still working on**

This tells you the task is not finished yet.

# 14. Speaking Pattern — Giving a Work Update

Use this simple structure:

### 1. What you completed

> “I finished ___.”

### 2. What you are doing now

> “I'm still working on ___.”

### 3. What remains

> “I still need to ___.”

### 4. Expected completion

> “I should have it done ___.”

---

## Example

> I finished the backend yesterday.  
> I'm still working on the frontend.  
> I still need to fix some validation.  
> I should have it done by the end of the day.

Keep your answer short.

You are giving a **meeting update**, not writing a report.

---

# 16. Response Practice

### Question

> “Where are we on this?”

Possible responses:

> “It's almost done.”

> “I'm still working on it.”

> “The backend is done, but the frontend is still in progress.”

> “It's mostly done. I just have a few things left.”

---

### Question

> “Are we still on track?”

Possible responses:

> “Yeah, I think so.”

> “Yes, we should be on track.”

> “Probably, as long as testing goes well.”

> “I'm not sure yet. I'll know more after testing.”

---

### Question

> “Anything you need from me?”

Possible responses:

> “No, I'm good for now.”

> “Yeah, I need the latest requirements.”

> “Could you check with QA?”

> “Not right now. I'll let you know if anything comes up.”
