# Lesson 03 — Asking for Clarification

## 1. Lesson Overview

**Target Level:** Level 1  
**Target WPM:** 110–130 WPM  
**Meeting Type:** Requirement Discussion  
**IT Context:** Web Development / Feature Requirement  
**Characters:** 2  
**Conversation Length:** 12–16 turns

### Main Goal

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Recognize when someone is explaining a requirement.
- Ask someone to repeat or clarify information.
- Ask about an unfamiliar requirement.
- Check the meaning of a specific word or phrase.
- Confirm what you understood.
- Avoid pretending to understand when you are unsure.

### Core Skill

> **Don't understand → Ask → Clarify → Confirm**

The goal is not to understand every sentence immediately.

The goal is to know what to do **when you don't understand**.

---

# 2. Situation

Sarah, the Project Manager, is explaining a new requirement for the checkout page.

David, the Developer, understands most of the requirement, but one part is unclear.

He needs to ask Sarah for clarification before implementing the feature.

---

# 3. IT Context

The team is working on an e-commerce website.

The client wants to change the checkout process.

One requirement is that users should be able to save their billing information.

However, David is not sure whether the information should be saved automatically or only after the user checks an option.

This is a common situation in real development meetings:

> The developer understands 80–90% of the requirement, but one detail is unclear.

Instead of guessing, David asks.

# 5. Business Meeting Patterns

## Asking someone to repeat

### “Sorry, could you say that again?”

= Xin lỗi, bạn có thể nói lại được không?

---

### “Sorry, I didn't catch that.”

= Xin lỗi, tôi không nghe rõ / không bắt được ý đó.

This is very useful in real meetings.

**catch** here does not mean physically catch something.

---

## Asking for clarification

### “Could you clarify that?”

= Bạn có thể làm rõ phần đó không?

---

### “What do you mean by that?”

= Ý bạn với phần đó là gì?

---

### “Just to clarify...”

= Để tôi xác nhận/làm rõ một chút...

This is useful when you understand most of the discussion but want to confirm one detail.

---

# 6. Confirming What You Understand

After getting an explanation, don't simply say:

> “Okay.”

You can confirm your understanding.

### “So, you mean...?”

= Vậy ý bạn là... đúng không?

### “So, if I understand correctly...”

= Nếu tôi hiểu đúng thì...

### “Just to make sure...”

= Để chắc chắn rằng tôi hiểu đúng...

---

# 7. Natural Meeting English

In a real meeting, people often say:

> “Sorry, I didn't catch that.”

instead of:

> “I did not understand what you said.”

The first one sounds much more natural in conversation.

---

Another useful expression:

> “What do you mean by ‘save’?”

This is more specific than simply saying:

> “I don't understand.”

You are telling the speaker exactly what you need clarified.

---

# 8. Natural / Reduced Speech

Pay attention to these expressions in the audio.

### “Could you say that again?”

The words may be connected in natural speech.

Try to recognize the whole phrase:

> **say that again**

---

### “I didn't catch that.”

Focus on:

> **didn't catch**

The phrase means:

> “I didn't hear/understand that.”

---

### “Just to clarify...”

This phrase is often spoken quickly because it is a familiar meeting pattern.

Do not wait for every individual word.

Recognize:

> **Just to clarify...**

as a signal that the speaker is about to confirm something.

# 9. Conversation

### Sarah — Project Manager

Hey David, for the new checkout, we want users to save their billing information.

### David — Developer

Sorry, I didn't catch that last part. Can you say it again?

### Sarah

Sure. Users should be able to save their billing information during checkout.

### David

Okay. Do you mean we save it automatically?

### Sarah

Not exactly. They should be able to choose whether to save it.

### David

Ah, okay. So we need a checkbox for that?

### Sarah

Yeah, that's what we're thinking.

### David

Got it. And should the checkbox be selected by default?

### Sarah

No, it should be unchecked by default.

### David

Okay, just to clarify, users have to check it before we save their information, right?

### Sarah

Exactly.

### David

Alright, that makes sense.

### Sarah

Cool. Let me know if anything else is unclear.

### David

Sure, will do.

# 14. Speaking Pattern — Asking for Clarification

When you don't understand, use this structure:

### Step 1 — Signal

> “Sorry...”

### Step 2 — Ask

> “I didn't catch that.”

or:

> “Could you clarify that?”

### Step 3 — Be specific

> “Do you mean we save it automatically?”

### Step 4 — Confirm

> “So, if I understand correctly...”

---

## Example

> Sorry, I didn't catch that.  
> Could you say it again?  
> Do you mean the system saves it automatically?

This is much better than staying silent and guessing.

---

# 15. Speaking Practice

Imagine Sarah explains this requirement:

> “The checkout should show the shipping option based on the customer's location.”

You are not completely sure what that means.

Ask Sarah for clarification.

Try to ask:

1. What determines the shipping option?
2. Which location should the system use?
3. What happens if there are multiple options?

Keep your questions short.

---

# 16. Response Practice

### You didn't hear something

> “Sorry, I didn't catch that.”

> “Sorry, could you say that again?”

---

### You don't understand a word

> “What do you mean by ‘shipping option’?”

---

### You understand most of it but need confirmation

> “So, you mean the system should choose it automatically?”

---

### You want to confirm everything

> “Just to clarify, we only show this option for logged-in users, right?”

# 17. AI Role-play

## Scenario

You are **David**, the Developer.

The AI is **Sarah**, the Project Manager.

Sarah explains a new e-commerce requirement.

You understand most of it, but some details are intentionally unclear.

Your job is to:

- Ask Sarah to repeat information when necessary.
- Ask specific clarification questions.
- Confirm your understanding.
- Avoid pretending you understand everything.

### AI Rules

The AI should:

- Explain one requirement.
- Include one or two ambiguous details.
- Give natural meeting responses.
- Wait for the user to ask clarification questions.
- Answer the questions naturally.
- Ask a follow-up question after clarification.

### Your Goal

Do not simply say:

> “Okay, I understand.”

Make sure you actually understand the requirement before agreeing to implement it.
